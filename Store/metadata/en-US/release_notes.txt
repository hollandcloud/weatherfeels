Turn your iPhone or iPad upright and the forecast now plays on a television.

• The set. In portrait, the picture sits on a CRT in a dark room instead of being
  stretched up the screen. Its buttons work: settings, back and forward through the
  rotation, and a power button that switches the tube off the way it used to.
• Three finishes. Studio monitor grey, wood veneer, or black plastic.
• Rotation. The app opens in landscape, filling the screen with the picture. Turn the
  device for the television. If you would rather have the set in both orientations,
  there is now a switch for that.
• The tube, by default. The CRT screen effect and medium scanlines are on out of the
  box on iPhone and iPad.
• The whole rotation, by default. Hourly Forecast, Travel, the hourly graph and the
  severe-weather outlook used to start switched off. Everything is on now; turn off
  what you do not want in Settings.

Fixed
• Current Conditions printed its labels through its readings on narrower layouts —
  "Pressure:" and the value ran into each other.
• The Hourly Forecast wind column lost its last digit at the right edge.
• The first-run screens had text that came out dark on the blue background in light
  mode, and was close to unreadable.
