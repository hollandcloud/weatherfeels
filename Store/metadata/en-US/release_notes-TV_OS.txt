• The Local Radar shows the weather where it actually is. It is drawn from a sharper
  national composite over a crisp map, with nearby towns, your location in yellow and a
  colour legend. The old picture threw away most of the radar returns and stretched what
  was left, which is why it looked like scattered dots.

• Weather bulletins. When the National Weather Service issues a warning for your location,
  the rotation breaks in with a full-screen bulletin and a chime, the way the WeatherStar
  did, and switches the set on if it is in standby. Watches and advisories stay on the
  Hazards display. Both the bulletin and its chime can be turned off in Settings.

• Local on the 8s. Turn it on in Settings, and at every minute ending in 8 the rotation
  cuts to your local forecast, then picks up where it left off.

• Tides. On the coast, a Tides display shows today's and tomorrow's high and low water at
  the nearest NOAA station. Inland, it stays out of the rotation.

• Local ads of your own, if you want them, all off until you switch them on. Type lines
  for the bottom ticker in Settings, or point the app at a companion server you run
  yourself for full-screen local ad cards and a reel of clips for commercial breaks.
  Nothing is fetched from a server until you turn one of these on.
